Does Your Business Need a Website or Social Media?

On October 4, 2021, Facebook's services went down for six hours because of an error made while updating what's known as the Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) — the system responsible for routing Facebook's traffic and making it reachable to visitors.

It was the worst outage Facebook had experienced since 2019, when the platform went dark for 24 hours. Millions of small businesses and creators whose daily work depended on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, and Oculus felt it immediately.

The Impact of the Facebook Outage

When Facebook's services went down, creators and small business owners around the world lost anywhere from a few hundred dollars up to $5,000. Many felt their sales weaken because they'd lost touch with customers in a way that was different from before.

Meanwhile, customers turned to other platforms, and other social networks got a real boost — Telegram alone gained 70 million users, and usage of apps like Snapchat, Twitter, Signal, and TikTok rose by as much as 23%.

Five days later, on October 9, Facebook and Instagram went down again. The repeated outages left customers questioning how much they could trust social media for their business — and pushed a lot of people back toward the open web: their own websites.

What Value Does Social Media Bring to Your Business?

Social media is still one of the most important marketing tools you have. It makes marketing and communication easier by delivering your business directly to wherever your customers already are — for engaging customers, advertising, selling, and keeping an eye on competitors.

But the Facebook outages made one thing clear: even the biggest platforms can fail, and when they do, you have zero control over the outcome.

The Benefits of a Business Website

Small businesses increased their online engagement by 51% in the aftermath of the Facebook crisis — most of them by building their own website. Here's how a website helps:

1. It increases brand awareness

Websites tend to rank higher in search results than social pages. Bright Local reports that 93% of people rely on internet search to find local businesses — and 53% of all website traffic comes from organic search. That's a lot of potential customers you simply can't reach through a social profile alone.

2. It gives you control over your business

You own and manage your own website, so you can arrange it exactly how you want, grow it without worrying about the next algorithm change, and use features that just aren't available on social platforms.

3. It opens up more revenue streams

A website lets you start an online store, sell ad space, become an affiliate marketer, or build a membership site — none of which a social profile alone can do.

4. It doesn't cost much

Plenty of small businesses skip having a website assuming it's expensive or technical. It isn't — you can have a WordPress site live on solid hosting for just a few dollars a month.

Website vs. Social Media — Which Is Better for Your Business?

The 2021 Facebook outages made the risk obvious. Social media lets you reach and sell to customers in ways a website can't replicate on its own — but it comes with a lack of control and a dependency on a company that owes you nothing.

The honest answer: don't choose one. Protect your business by running both — a website you fully own, and social media to extend its reach — each one covering what the other can't.
