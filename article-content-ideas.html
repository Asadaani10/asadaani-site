18 Content Ideas When You Don't Know What to Write

If you're a new website owner, content marketing is the simplest and most affordable marketing strategy there is. It helps people find your business name, builds trust with readers, and makes it easy to see where you need to improve.

Ideally, every business website publishes something new weekly, or a few times a week. But coming up with fresh ideas is genuinely difficult. Here are 18 that consistently work.

1. Your business goals

Start the year with fresh momentum — write about what you're aiming for, new programs, or updated policies worth sharing.

2. Discounted items

Discount older products to make room for new ones. A seasonal promo on a portion of your catalog is an easy, recurring post.

3. Recaps

Summarize recent products, news, or articles, highlighting the most important ones — readers scan a recap far more easily than they sift through everything at once.

4. Feature a well-known figure

Tie a public figure your audience already follows to a topic you cover often. Relevance matters more than fame.

5. Run a contest

A simple giveaway — like, share, comment to enter — is a reliable way to generate short-term buzz.

6. Cover what's new in your market

Set up Google Alerts for your niche so you're first to share updates, with a link back to your site for the full story.

7. Highlight an award or recognition

Recognizing a top customer or team member publicly builds real goodwill with your community.

8. Make tutorial or training videos

Show people how to use your product, or share a customer success story — video connects in ways text can't.

9. Write book reviews

Share a book that genuinely helped you, and what you took from it.

10. Share quotes

A simple "quote of the day" from thought leaders in your niche is easy to produce and easy to share.

11. Run interviews

Interview a team member, customer, or expert in your niche — especially anyone whose story connects to your product.

12. Update your key pages

Refresh your contact page, product reviews, and FAQs whenever something changes — and feature real customer feedback while you're at it.

13. Lean into seasonal moments

Not every post needs to be about your business directly — seasonal, lighter content keeps readers engaged without feeling like an ad.

14. Host a Q&A

Collect real customer questions and answer them in a post or short video.

15. Start a podcast

A monthly episode answering customer questions is a low-lift way to build a second content channel.

16. Point readers to useful resources

A "best of" roundup for your local area or niche gives readers value even when it's not directly about you.

17. Share job-search tips

If your audience is job-hunting, market trends and resume tips are consistently high-engagement topics.

18. Use user-generated content

An Instagram campaign inviting customers to submit photos with your product does double duty: content, and social proof.

Bonus idea: Turn a deep-dive topic into a downloadable PDF or e-book, offered free in exchange for an email sign-up. It's one of the highest-leverage content formats for building a mailing list.

There are plenty of ways to find ideas — the key is understanding what you're genuinely good at, then reaching your audience through the most affordable channels available: YouTube, a Facebook Page, Instagram, and similar platforms.
