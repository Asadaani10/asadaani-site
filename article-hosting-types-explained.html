Shared vs VPS vs Dedicated vs WordPress Hosting, Explained

Every website running on the internet needs somewhere to live. Before you buy hosting, it's worth understanding what the different types actually do — and which one fits where you are right now.

What Is Web Hosting?

Short answer: it's where your website's files and data are stored on the internet. You're renting a small plot of internet real estate for your site to sit on, along with the infrastructure that keeps it reachable whenever someone types in your domain.

What to Check Before You Buy

Bandwidth & disk space

If your site has a lot of images, many pages, and heavy traffic, you'll need real capacity. Many providers now offer effectively unlimited plans on their mid-tier packages.

Compatibility

Double-check the operating system and stack (PHP version, database support) match what your site needs — switching later is a headache you can avoid by checking upfront.

Reliability (uptime)

The best hosts guarantee 99%+ availability. Anything below that and you're looking at real, customer-facing downtime.

Security

Firewalls, daily backups, and proper user authentication should be included by default — not an upsell.

The Main Hosting Types

Shared Hosting

Your site lives on the same server as many other websites, sharing memory and CPU with all of them. It's the most affordable option and works well for basic sites — email, file sharing, simple ecommerce. It's the right starting point for most small or new businesses.

VPS

A VPS gives you a private virtual server carved out of a shared physical machine. You get dedicated memory, processing power, and resources that aren't affected by other sites' traffic spikes. It's more reliable than shared hosting, at a fraction of dedicated hosting's cost.

Dedicated Hosting

You get an entire physical server to yourself, with full administrative control to configure it however you like. It's the most powerful option, and the most expensive — usually unnecessary unless you're running serious traffic or have specific compliance needs.

WordPress Hosting

A flavor of shared (or managed) hosting tuned specifically for WordPress — faster load times, pre-installed WordPress, WordPress-specific security, and automatic core updates. If your site runs on WordPress, this tier is usually worth the small premium.

What Actually Separates Good Hosts From Great Ones

Uptime guarantee

HostGator advertises one of the stronger uptime guarantees in the industry, at 99.98%. Whichever host you pick, treat the uptime guarantee as a floor, not a marketing line — check independent monitoring reports if you can.

Loading time

Page speed affects both how quickly Google can crawl and index you, and how likely a visitor is to stick around. Slow hosting quietly kills conversions before you even notice. Hosting.com is worth a look here if performance is your top priority.

Customer support

When something breaks — and eventually something will — you want support that's fast, knowledgeable, and easy to reach. DreamHost consistently rates well on this front.

Server location

The closer your server is to your visitors, the faster your site loads for them. Hostinger runs data centers across multiple regions, which is worth checking if your audience is spread across countries.

Downtime

Beyond the headline uptime number, look at how a host handles outages when they do happen — status pages, communication, and compensation policy all matter more than people realize.
