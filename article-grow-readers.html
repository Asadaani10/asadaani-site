4 Strategies to Help You Get More Readers

Building and launching your website is only part of the job. What do you actually do once you've published your first post — and how do you turn occasional visitors into readers who keep coming back?

You can have the best content in the world, but if you want it read, you need to know how to reach people better than everyone else in your market does. Here are four strategies that consistently move the needle.

1. Invite guests

Reaching out to established names in your niche — people who already rank well and have an audience — is one of the fastest ways to attract new readers. Expect early rejections; keep going anyway. The key is finding where your readers already spend time, and showing up there yourself.

2. Publish consistently, on-topic

Stay tightly focused on what your blog is actually about. Before committing to a niche, ask yourself:

  • Which topics have I genuinely enjoyed reading myself?
  • How many monthly readers could this niche realistically bring? (A traffic-estimation tool gives you a rough baseline.)
  • Does content in this space typically get real engagement — shares, comments — or does it fall flat?

Once you've published a few pieces that land well, the ideas start flowing much more easily, and reach compounds from there.

3. Use social media deliberately, not everywhere

Trying to be active on every platform spreads you too thin. Pick one — two at most — where your specific audience actually is, and go deep instead of wide. As a rough guide to what tends to work where:

  • Facebook: video and curated content
  • Instagram: photos, quotes, stories
  • Twitter/X: news and short-form posts
  • LinkedIn: professional content
  • Pinterest: infographics and visual guides
  • Reddit: genuine discussion in your niche

Test, then commit to what's actually working for you rather than spreading effort evenly across all of them.

4. Join groups and forums

Active participation in a relevant community — a Facebook group, a subreddit, a forum — often returns more than social media does, especially early on. Give real value before you ever mention your own site; the readers and collaborators come from the relationships, not the link drops.

The throughline: none of these four strategies work as one-off tactics. They compound when you stay consistent — and consistency is a lot easier when your site itself isn't fighting you with slow load times or downtime.

Attracting a loyal readership isn't easy, but it isn't complicated either: be genuine, stay on-topic, and show up consistently. The rest follows.
